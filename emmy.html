THE_GATHERING
In the profound silence that followed a metaphorical winter, I wandered through a desert of my own making. After the demise of a long marriage, my spirit felt substantially crushed; I moved through the world with a heart held cautiously aloof, navigating relationships that never quite took root, often retreating into my own self-sabotage. I was a man who had accepted the cold, convinced that the "warm breeze" of true connection was a myth told by those who hadn't yet been weathered by the storm. But the universe holds its own timing, and it operates in mysterious search diameters. It was a quick weekend getaway to the California coast—a trip meant for the simple solace of a longboard and a boardwalk—that set our orbits on a collision course. I was an Arizona soul looking for the Pacific, and she was a California light I didn't yet know I was missing. Against the backdrop of a world beginning to shutter under the weight of a global pandemic, a Christian-based app called Mutual bridged the state lines I had sworn never to cross again. Having just emerged from a failed long-distance struggle, I was hesitant. I left our match "on read" for 6.95 days, hovering at the edge of another "almost," waiting for the clock to run out. Then, in the eleventh hour, I jumped. I sent the message, and what followed was not a mere introduction; it was a sudden, overwhelming, and "pleasantly violent" recognition. It was an answering cry between two spirits—a knowing that felt like being called home after years of exile. To meet Emily was to realize that while the world was closing its doors and restricting our travels, mine were finally being thrown open. I had met many remarkable women whose lights had been dimmed by the cruelties of life, but Emily was different. Her spirit remained undimmed, sparkling with a resilience and an empathy that seemed to defy the challenges she had faced. She became my "Elsa," the calm in every storm, bringing a limitless capacity for patience that I saw mirrored in the way she cared for her four daughters. Watching her exists in the world didn't just make me love her; it made me want to be a better father to my own three children. Our story was forged in the quiet rituals of a locked-down world—of watching WandaVision and Frozen while the rest of the world stood still. We are the youngest and the oldest, two halves of a long-sought whole who found each other when the math said we shouldn't have. Today, the desert has vanished, replaced by the harmony of a life built on shared promises. We are no longer wandering; we are "Kings and Queens" in a kingdom of our own making, proof that even after the longest winter, the sun eventually finds a way to break through.
