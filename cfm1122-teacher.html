I Will Love Them Freely

A 25-minute lesson about the Lord’s invitation to return, and what His patience can teach us about loving the people in our lives.

Open TV presentation ↗

Before class

Open the TV presentation, try the arrow keys, and check the sound on the podcast screen. The clip plays from 24:49 to 25:49. If the TV or internet is unavailable, teach from this page and read the short summary below.

If time is short, give the timeline in one sentence, read Hosea 14:1 and 4–7 and Jacob 5:15, 41, and 50–51, then use the family question and close with Joel. The other readings are there if the class wants to stay with them.

Timeline Hosea Jacob Clip Discuss Close

0–5 minutes

Make the jump from Daniel to Hosea

Opening screenThe two kingdomsTimeline

“Last week we were with Daniel in Babylon. Today we’re going back about 150 years, to when Israel and Judah were two kingdoms. Hosea is speaking to Israel in the north, before Assyria conquered it. Jerusalem, the capital of Judah, was still standing.”
After Solomon
The kingdom divides. Israel is in the north, with its capital at Samaria. Judah is in the south, with its capital at Jerusalem.
Mid-700s BC
Hosea warns northern Israel, also called Ephraim. Assyria captures Samaria around 722–721 BC and carries away many of its people.
Around 600 BC
Babylon takes people from Judah, including Daniel and his friends. Jerusalem and its temple fall around 587–586 BC.
After 539 BC
Persia takes Babylon. Cyrus lets Jewish exiles return and rebuild. They begin returning in the part of the story we read in Ezra and Nehemiah.

If someone asks about Joel, he speaks to Judah and Jerusalem. His date is uncertain. Hosea is the one we can place before Assyria’s conquest.

Hosea and Joel background · The two captivities

5–10 minutes

Hear the Lord’s invitation

Hosea screen

“Hosea compares Israel’s covenant with the Lord to a marriage. What stayed with me was how the Lord keeps inviting us to come back to Him. I’d just heard an interview about how to keep loving someone through a change in faith, and I kept thinking about the two together.”

Invite someone to read Hosea 14:1–7 aloud. Ask the class to listen for the invitation in verse 1 and the Lord’s answer in verse 4. Then notice what begins to grow.

“I will heal their backsliding, I will love them freely.”

What do the roots and branches add to the words “I will love them freely”?

If the class is quiet, ask them to name one detail they can picture. Dew gives the plant what it needs. Roots take hold, branches spread, and eventually others find shade there. Hosea is showing what healing might look like for Israel.

More context if it comes up

In Hosea 1–3, the marriage of Hosea and Gomer pictures the covenant between God and Israel. In Hosea 11:1–4, 8–9, the image changes to a parent who taught a child to walk and still loves that child. Read it if the class wants another picture of how God feels about His people. Hosea 11

10–15 minutes

Watch the gardener in Jacob 5

The tree starts to growThe gardener gives it time

Tell the class that Jacob quotes a prophet named Zenos. The olive tree represents Israel here, too. Invite readers for Jacob 5:3–6 and 15, then verse 41 and verses 50–51. Between the readings, explain that the gardener finds good fruit on one visit and later comes back to find the trees struggling again.

What do you notice about the way the gardener responds when the trees take time to grow or stop bearing good fruit?

“Hosea lets us picture Israel growing again. Jacob lets us see how much time, work, and care go into that growth. The gardener keeps coming back. In Jacob 6:4, Jacob says the Lord keeps reaching out to His people all day long.”
How Jacob 4 and 6 frame the story

Jacob 4:15–18 asks how people who have rejected Christ, the sure foundation, could ever build on Him again. Jacob 6:4–6 says God remembers Israel and keeps reaching out. Jacob then asks each of us to turn to God with our whole heart.

15–18 minutes

Play one minute of the interview

Podcast screenOpen on YouTube at 24:49

Introduce Joseph Grenny as a guest on Keystone talking about how families respond when someone steps away from the Church. Ask the class to listen for what he notices about the time between the gardener’s visits. The TV excerpt stops at 25:49. Click Next when it ends. If you open it on YouTube, pause it at 25:49.

Read this if playback is unavailable. “Grenny notices how many times Jacob says a long time has passed. The gardener prunes and nourishes the trees, then gives them time before coming back. Grenny hears something about patience in that story. We can keep caring about someone while giving them time and space to make their own choices.”

18–23 minutes

Keep the door open

Family discussion screen

In the wider interview, Grenny suggests taking a breath, naming what you feel, and asking what story you’re telling yourself. Feeling hurt is real. It can help to pause before deciding what the other person meant. Grenny on the stories we tell ourselves at 17:12.

Use the example on the screen. Ask the class to name the feeling and the assumption, then think of something they could say. Give people a few seconds to think.

What could you say that would let them know you love them and that they can talk to you when they’re ready?

If they need a starting point, try “I love you. I’m glad you told me. I’m here if you want to talk more.” Ask what it might look like to keep showing love after that first conversation.

If you want a short example from the interview, Grenny and his son agreed to quit taking digs at each other’s beliefs. Grenny started by admitting what he’d been doing himself. Hear that part at 45:34.

23–25 minutes

Bring it back to our own hearts

Closing screen

Read Joel 2:12–13. Joel asks for a real change of heart and reminds us that the Lord is gracious and slow to anger. Give the class a moment to think about His invitation to them personally.

Where is the Lord inviting me to come back? Who could I keep loving while I give them time?

Leave a quiet moment to think. Close in your own words about what gives you hope in the Savior’s willingness to keep caring for us.
