The Lord

A 20–25 minute conversation about the Lord’s care for His people and how we can share it.

Before class

Receive the message first.

What has this message meant to me before I ask it to mean something to anyone else?

Read Ezekiel 34:11–16. Consider when you’ve needed to be found, strengthened, or given room to recover. Is there a way someone helped you feel the Lord’s care? You don’t need to share your answers.

Plan: 3 minutes for the opening and exile; 4 for judgment and hope; 7 for the Shepherd and cards; 5 for the Savior’s understanding; 4 to close. That’s about 23 minutes, with room to adapt.

Opening + screens 01–02 · About 3 minutes

Far from home. Not beyond His reach.

Opening ↗01 · In exile ↗02 · Sat where they sat ↗

Ezekiel was among the people taken from Judah to Babylon around 597 BC. Later, Jerusalem and its temple were destroyed. While Ezekiel was still in exile, he saw the glory of the Lord in Babylon.

“They may have wondered: Has God abandoned us? Are we cut off? Is there any future for us?”

Read Ezekiel 3:15; the screen highlights “I sat where they sat.”

What stands out to you about Ezekiel sitting among the people?

Take one or two responses. Ezekiel shared their displacement; he was among the people he had been called to serve.

Screens 03–04 · About 4 minutes

“Turn … and live.”

03 · Turn and live ↗04 · Suffering and judgment ↗

A possible transition, if it feels right in your own words:

“I find some of Ezekiel’s language difficult, especially the warnings and descriptions of destruction. I’m still thinking about how to understand it. At the same time, I’m paying attention to what Ezekiel also shows me about the Lord seeking and healing His people.”

Invite someone to read Ezekiel 33:10–11 aloud. The screen shows selected phrases; the complete verses are in the linked scripture.

What do you hear in the Lord’s response to people who feel hopeless?

Briefly refer to Luke 13:1–5. Jesus says those who died in tragedies were not greater sinners than others; He invites His listeners to repent. This does not settle every question about judgment and suffering. It gives us a reason to be cautious about interpreting a person’s suffering as evidence of their guilt.

If the class wants to stay with this question, listen and allow the tension to remain. Avoid assigning guilt to people because they suffered.

Screens 05–07 · About 7 minutes

The Lord notices both.

05 · The Shepherd ↗06 · Both ↗07 · Scenario cards ↗

Invite someone to read Ezekiel 34:11–12 and the first part of verse 16. The screens show selected phrases.

What do these words tell you about how the Lord responds to His sheep?

Notice the distinction between the sheep that is lost and the one driven away. Their stories may be different. The Lord notices both. The passage also speaks of binding up the broken and strengthening the sick.

Choose a scenario card

Ask the class to choose one of the three cards. Click it to open the situation and the discussion question on the TV. There are no points or right answers.

  • Someone feels distant from God and isn’t sure how to begin again.
  • Someone has been hurt or pushed away in a place where they hoped to belong.
  • Someone is carrying a private burden that others don’t understand.

Without assuming we know the whole story, what might it look like to care for this person as the Savior would?

If useful: What might we listen for? What could we say or do? What assumptions might get in the way?

The Lord says He will seek His sheep. He also calls people to care for one another. We can be His hands and feet through listening, making amends, offering help, or respecting someone’s space.

The scenarios are invitations to consider care, not diagnoses of anyone in the room. No one needs to share a personal experience.

Screens 08–10 · About 5 minutes

He knows the whole person.

08 · Known by the Savior ↗09 · Desires of our hearts ↗10 · A new heart ↗

Invite someone to read Alma 7:11–12. The screen shows a selected phrase from verse 11.

Then read Doctrine and Covenants 137:9, which speaks of the Lord judging according to our works and the desires of our hearts.

How might we respond differently if we remember what the Savior knows about someone and what we don’t?

We come into mortality without a full memory of our premortal life, into different circumstances and with different burdens. We see only part of another person’s life. Christ knows more than we do.

If time allows, read Ezekiel 36:26. The Lord’s promise of a new heart speaks of renewal within us, too.

Screen 11 · About 4 minutes

A quiet invitation.

11 · Closing screen ↗

Give the class a few quiet moments to think. No one needs to share.

Where do I need the Lord’s care? Who could I notice or care for with more understanding this week?

Close in your own words. You might return to the thought that the Savior sees the whole person and continues to seek His sheep.

“He knows when someone is lost, and He knows when someone has been driven away.”
