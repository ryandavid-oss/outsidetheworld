# Wow...that came out of nowhere.
Date: March 13, 2026

​**Hello, and welcome** to Friday, weary travelers.  

My sparkling hope for this week has been that at least some of you have stopped by from my social media posts.  Of course, there's also my t-shirt giveaway, and I hope that everyone will have a chance to find the secret and submit an entry. These shirts are the prettiest purple I could find, and you'll be one of only three humans to have it if you win the random drawing.

**In other news:** my week off of work has come to an end as of 5 p.m. today. I can't remember the last time I spent a full 40 hours of PTO to just rest, reset, and relax, but I heartily recommend it to those who can afford it. I remember when I was 17 years old and living with my brother Tyler in his tiny apartment in Bountiful, Utah...on a drive out to pick up something in Salt Lake City (I cannot remember what it was now), he told me in the proudest of terms that he was getting paid while hanging out with me.  I recall thinking how absolutely fabulous that seemed, and forever thereafter, I've recorded the time that I get paid on vacation with silent pleasure.

At the other end of that habitual pattern persists the fact that I also become acutely aware of when my paid vacation ends, and here is where I find myself chaotically repeating myself. Time to prepare to get back into the office.

Honestly, I am ready to return. I am one of the rare and fortunate individuals who value their job and the people in it. Consequently, my unusually quiet week has been filled with vivid dreams of people randomly relocating my office as a form of punishment for my absence, passionately criticizing my recent job performance, or returning to work and not recognizing the environment or the employees. As a person possessing an unnatural amount of baseline anxiety, this is not a particularly healthy mental state in which to persist longer than a week.

**Speaking of my dear brother Tyler,** I sadly realize that the anniversary of his passing creeps up steadily. To know that he has been gone for nearly two full years now brings more than a momentary touch of melancholy. Not a month goes by where I don't dream vividly of his presence, where normal conversation flows for what feels like hours until the torturous machinations of my conscious forbid me further discussion, and he is torn from me as I become aware of the inconsistency and open my eyes to the quiet of my dark room. He and I share much, much more than a simple last name; I feel I carry within me the infused blood of every anxious thought and turbulent lonely night he experienced on Earth.

I more than empathize with him; I would carry his mortal frailty upon my back if I could only have one more phone call with him as we did upon my separation from my first marriage. It is truly one of my most cherished memories of him, where he, in his stuttered, imperfect diction, told me he knew exactly how I felt. And in that moment, at the base of the stairwell leading up to my empty apartment, I knew I had a companion in this world with more than platitudes and loving gestures. I had a molecularly bound man of flesh and blood who I could turn to and cry out my frustrations and loneliness to, and it would be received with compassion, understanding, and solidarity. 

He was a unique man, a wholly imperfect and disastrously vexed man of conflicting emotions, outbursts, prayers, and effort. I think of him often. As the first of my immediate family to depart this mortal sphere, he will always hold such a sacred capacity in my soul, a Pandora's box of friction and contradictory sensations. As is apt in stories such as these, which I am sure many of you dear readers can empathize with, I am certain of the fact that I never really told him how much I appreciated him. In those rare occasions where communication was attempted, either one of us afflicted the tone of the moment with stubborn denial and pseudo-modesty.

**One thing is for certain:** these recurring dreams are not of the normal persuasion. They are laced with intention, carrying within them the hallmarks of that blessed state when the mortal veil parts ever so slightly, and we imperfect souls are able to dance tremulously with the divine. It bears witness to my soul that he has gone on, but not departed. His lifelong struggles with mental illness have evaporated, leaving him able to continue his path of fatherhood and his infectious desire to learn and serve others. 

As the day that marks his passing approaches closer, I am sure that I will recall more about his life and legacy, and ponder yet more upon the similarities between him and me.
