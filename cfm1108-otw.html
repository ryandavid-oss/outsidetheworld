The Lord
notices both.

Ezekiel · A few thoughts before we meet

The Church has given us a lot to study in Ezekiel this week. I wanted to share a few thoughts that stood out to me as I read, and some questions I hope we can discuss on Sunday.

Explore this week’s Come, Follow Me lesson ↗

As I read about Ezekiel and the people living in exile, I kept coming back to a thought: they were not beyond God’s reach.

I imagine they might have wondered whether they were cut off, whether God had abandoned them, and whether there was any future for them.

I also find some of Ezekiel’s language difficult. The warnings and descriptions of destruction raise questions for me about how we understand God’s judgment and the suffering people experience. I’m still thinking about that.

Something else stood out to me in Ezekiel 33:10–16. Our past does not have to determine our future. The Lord invites us to turn, make things right, and live. Past goodness doesn’t excuse the harm we might do now, and past mistakes don’t mean we can never change.

Later in Ezekiel 33:30–33, Ezekiel describes people who listen to his words but don’t act on them. That made me stop and think. It is possible to enjoy hearing a message from God without letting it change how we live.

Ezekiel 34

Lost.
Driven away.
The Lord notices both.

In Ezekiel 34, the Lord speaks of seeking the lost and bringing back those who have been driven away. He promises to bind up the broken and strengthen the sick.

Those can be very different experiences. The Lord notices both.

Read Ezekiel 34:11–16 ↗

I’ve long believed that we are the Lord’s hands and feet here on earth. As I read these verses, I’ve been thinking about what it means to help Him seek and care for His sheep. We may know so little about what someone else has been carrying.

A question for Sunday

What do these verses help you understand about the way the Lord cares for His people?

We’ll talk more about this together on Sunday.
