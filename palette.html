Typography system

Current OTW type voices

Primary Sans

Inter

Clean public-facing body copy, modern UI text, and readable utility language across OTW and Professional.

A quarter-century of digital residue keeps finding its way back to the signal.

Best for: body copy, navigation, modern utility text, calmer public surfaces.

Signal Mono

Fira Code

Instrument-panel text, timestamps, coordinates, labels, and anything that should feel live, coded, or operational.

LIVE_NOW // QUEEN_CREEK_SIGNAL 03_31_2026 // BROADCAST_LOCKED

Best for: metadata, system labels, OTW signal language, technical framing.

Brand Sans

IBM Plex Sans

More authored and technical than Inter. Used heavily in core OTW brand pages, fragments surfaces, and private app lanes.

The suite stays calm, precise, and atmospheric even when the work itself is complicated.

Best for: OTW-branded UI, denser layouts, app shells, and structured editorial surfaces.

Brand Mono

IBM Plex Mono

The strongest OTW coded identity font. Carries panel labels, chips, toggles, queue states, and launcher language.

QUEUE_STATUS: CLEAR GHOST_WRITER // READY FRAGMENTS // LIVE

Best for: OTW app chrome, cards, section labels, control surfaces, internal tools.

Editorial Serif

IBM Plex Serif

Reflective OTW serif voice. It adds gravity and humanity without drifting out of the system.

Returned signal from the sleeping mind.

Best for: article titles, reflective intros, pull-quote energy, public OTW editorial moments.

Word.Person Display

Fraunces

Warm, intimate, devotional, and image-led. This is the signature display voice for Word.Person and Drift-adjacent poetry surfaces.

A room made of light, paper, and wanting.

Best for: emotional display copy, poetry titles, sanctuary/editorial lanes, feminine warmth.

Archive Serif

Merriweather

Used most clearly in the modern residue archive. It gives archive reading a quieter literary depth than the main OTW serif set.

The archive does not just remember; it re-frames, softens, and returns.

Best for: archive reading surfaces, longer-form literary bodies, slower reflective text.
